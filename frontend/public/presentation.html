01

The Threat

Private Equity Is Squeezing
Youth Sports for Profit

Youth sports is a $40 billion industry — and private equity has noticed. In just the last few years, PE firms have poured billions into acquiring the platforms clubs depend on. KKR bought Varsity Brands for $4.75B. EQT acquired IMG Academy for $1.25B. Waud Capital took over TeamSnap and rolled up 8 companies. Genstar Capital merged PlayMetrics and Stack Sports. SportsEngine is reportedly on the block for $400–500M.1

The result? Prices go up, platforms get bloated, and the organizations closest to kids pay more for tools that weren't built for them. Family costs have jumped 46% since 2019 — now averaging over $1,000 per child per year.2

The Problem

Running a Youth Sports Club
Shouldn't Require 6+ Tools

Club admins and coaches spend more time wrestling software than coaching athletes.

Roster Chaos

Spreadsheets, paper sign-ups, and email threads to manage who's on which team.

Scattered Communications

GroupMe for one team, text chains for another, email blasts from a personal Gmail.

Payment Headaches

Venmo requests, paper checks, and no clear picture of who's paid and who hasn't.

Parent Frustration

Parents check 4 different apps to find the schedule, pay a fee, and RSVP to practice.

Zero Visibility

No single dashboard for registrations, revenue, compliance, or engagement.

Tournament Nightmares

Brackets on whiteboards, scores texted in, standings updated manually in Sheets.

02

The Real Cost

Bad Software Doesn't Just
Waste Time — It Ruins the Experience

When the tools don't work, the stress doesn't stay with the admin. It flows downhill — to coaches, to parents, to kids. And when the experience breaks down, people walk away.

Coaches Burn Out

Volunteers who signed up to coach are drowning in admin work — chasing payments, managing spreadsheets, sending texts one by one. The passion that got them started gets buried under busywork.

Parents Get Frustrated

Missed schedules, confusing payment requests, no idea where to find info. Parents lose trust in the club. Frustration turns into complaints — or worse, they quietly pull their kids out.

Kids Stop Playing

When clubs can't operate smoothly, programs shrink, fees climb, and families leave. The kids who need sports the most — for confidence, discipline, and belonging — are the first to lose access.

This isn't just a software problem. It's hurting youth sports around the globe.

03

Architecture

Built-In Role-Based Access

Every user sees exactly what they should. Enforced server-side, not just in the UI.

Club Admin

Full visibility and control across the organization.

  • All teams, athletes, and parents
  • Club-wide communications
  • Revenue dashboard & financials
  • Template library management
  • Tournament creation
  • Volunteer compliance oversight

Coach

Scoped to assigned teams — data stays in their lane.

  • Own team rosters only
  • Team-scoped communications
  • Calendar & attendance
  • Use templates (can't create)
  • Tryout evaluations
  • Team-level reporting

Parent

Self-service portal for their family only.

  • Own children's profiles only
  • Payments & invoices
  • Schedules, RSVP, medical info
  • Team chat
  • Volunteer signup
  • Documents & waivers
